### Why it worked

- **One concrete scene.** It's August in Santurce, the AC is broken, the humidity is 90%, a downpour hits a zinc roof, and a ceiling fan turns. Every image is something you can feel or hear on the island.
- **The hook word has a double meaning.** *Humedá*, the island's humidity and her wetness, is spelled the way it's said.
- **Wordplay comes from a local sense of a word.** *El aire* is the AC in PR, which makes *"no pase ni el aire"* and *"que se joda el aire"* land.
- **It's mutual.** It's just the two of them, both into it, with no third party.
- **The language is spoken, not poetic.**
  - dialogue: *"Acho, qué calor"*
  - a PR question: *¿qué tú 'tás esperando?*
  - plain vulgarity: *chingándote, culo*
- **Every hook line rhymes on -á:** mojá, sudá, pegá, ma, humedá. The verses rhyme on -ao: dañao, entrao, amarrao, sudao.
- **Each verse has a punchline.** In verse 1 it's *"pues quítatelo"*; in verse 2 the coquí goes quiet to listen.
- **There's a callback.** The t-shirt in verse 1 is soaked on the floor in verse 2, and the fan in the hook sets the rhythm in verse 2.
- **The ending flips the hook.** *Ya escampó allá afuera… aquí adentro, no.*
- **The word count is low.** It leaves space for pads and reverb.

## Drafts that missed, and the lesson from each

1. **"Satélite"** (poetic, then an explicit rewrite). The user quoted *"Si me suelta' esta noche / Yo me pierdo en lo' nublao'"* as "still off".
   - **Lesson:** metaphors like satellites and clouds sound written, not spoken. Adding vulgar words to a poetic premise didn't fix it.
2. **"Vidrio' Arriba"** (a car with the windows up, smoke, *"tu jevo en la casa"*). The user's verdict: "Don't feel like it's good yet."
   - **Lesson:** generic trap imagery plus a cheating premise.
3. **"Ojalá Que No Vuelva"** (a blackout night, where her boyfriend thinks she's at her mom's). It had strong local details: the generator, LUMA, the barrio yelling *"¡llegó la luz!"*. The premise was still cheating, and the user changed the process after it.
   - **Lesson:** good specifics can't save a premise built on a third party.
4. **"Aborrecío"** (flex vs. emptiness, and his ex is now with a crypto gringo in Dorado). The user's verdict: "Can be sexual but not about cheating. Start over."
   - **Lesson:** a third party again, and too much backstory.
5. **"Humedá"** was approved.

### The pattern

Every miss had a third person in it or was written like a poem. The hit was a single specific scene between two people, spoken plainly, with a double-meaning hook. Start there by default.

## Lessons to add later

When the user states a new lasting preference, add it here with the date and the exact wording they used, so future songs follow it.

- **2026-10-23, "Brilla":** *"This is great and i like it. It's just that its just like the other song you made earlier. So maybe make the skill read the other previous song files so its not too similar lyrically."*
  - **Rule:** before writing, read every earlier song (`song-log.md`, `*-lyrics.md` files, this conversation) and make the new one different on every fingerprint row, not just the scene. Use "Humedá" to calibrate the voice, never as a template. Its structure, intro, hook shape, outro flip and phrases are used up.
