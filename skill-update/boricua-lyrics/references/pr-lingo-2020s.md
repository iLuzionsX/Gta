# Puerto Rican Lingo in 2020s Urban Music

*How people on the island talk now, and how to write it into lyrics (2020–2026)*

These notes draw on:
- Puerto Rican slang references
- linguistics research on Puerto Rican Spanish
- press coverage of the 2025 *DtMF* dictionary
- how the current PR wave writes (Bad Bunny, Jhayco, Eladio Carrión, Mora, Álvaro Díaz, Rauw Alejandro, Young Miko, Omar Courtz)

The lessons from writing and revising songs with the user are folded in too. Sources are at the end. No lyrics are quoted; every example line is original.

## 1. The quick checklist

Before calling a lyric "boricua", check these ten things:

1. It sounds like a text message or a voice note, not a poem.
2. It has at least one hyper-local detail: a place, a brand, or a PR problem (apagón, LUMA, Dorado gringos, el Choli, the humidity).
3. It rhymes by vowel sound (assonance), and the same ending runs for 4 or more lines.
4. Words are clipped where that helps the flow: pa', to', na', toa, -ao / -á / -ía / -ío.
5. "Tú" is said out loud and often, even in questions: *¿Qué tú quieres?*
6. Each section has 1–3 English nouns (story, DM, shot, t-shirt). Never a full English sentence unless it's the hook.
7. Each verse has one punchline or joke, even in a sad song.
8. Vulgarity is casual, the way a friend talks, not stacked for shock.
9. Nothing slips in from Mexican, Colombian, Dominican or Spain slang (see §7).
10. The hook is one phrase you could repeat four times without getting tired of it.

## 2. Sound: how to spell the accent

Only spell the features that change rhythm or rhyme. The singer's delivery handles the rest.

| Feature | Write it as | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **s dropped or aspirated** (end of a syllable) | ere', tú sabe', la' gata', ehto, 'tamo, má' | This is the most important one. Plurals often disappear. |
| **d dropped** in -ado / -ada / -ido / -ad | pegao, sudao, mojá, sudá, empapá, prendía, prendío, callaíto, humedá, veldá | Gives you ready-made rhyme chains (-ao, -á, -ía, -ío). |
| **Clipping** | pa', to', toa (*toda*), na', 'tá, 'toy, 'tamo, pa'lante | |
| **r → l** (lambdacism, at the end of a syllable) | pol, veldá, amol, Nueva Yol | Bad Bunny titled two 2025 songs "NUEVAYoL" and "VeLDÁ". Use it once or twice per song at most, or it becomes a caricature. |
| **Questions without inversion** | ¿Qué tú quieres? ¿Dónde tú 'tás? ¿Qué tú 'tás esperando? | In one study of Puerto Rican conversation, about 25% of question-word questions with a stated subject kept subject-before-verb order. Standard Spanish rejects *¿Qué tú quieres?*, so this marks the voice as boricua right away. |
| **"Tú" said out loud** | Tú sabe' que tú ere' mía | Caribbean Spanish states subject pronouns far more than other varieties. |
| **rr in the throat** | (don't spell it) | Rural and older speakers. It's a delivery choice, not a spelling one. |

## 3. Spanglish: the signature of this decade

### Adapted: Spanish verbs built from English (these sound the most boricua)

| Word | From | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| janguear / jangueo | hang out | go out / the going-out scene |
| gufear | goofy | mess around |
| pichear | pitcher (baseball) | ignore someone, blow it off |
| tripear | trip | tease, joke with someone |
| bichote | big shot | the boss, the big player |
| broki | brother | bro |
| pana | partner | friend |
| parquear / parquiao | park | park / parked |
| ghostear (me ghosteó) | ghost | ghost someone |
| stalkear | stalk | stalk someone online |
| kloufrens | close friends | Instagram's Close Friends list (also a Bad Bunny song title) |
| girla | girl | girl (used on *DtMF*) |

### Unadapted: English words dropped in as-is

- **Heard on *DtMF*:** shot, flashback, follow, bestie
- **Everyday youth Spanglish:** baby, story, DM, t-shirt (*la t-shirt*), vibe, flow, outfit, red flag, random, literal

**Rule:** English goes into nouns, tags and ad-libs. Spanish carries the verbs and the feeling.

## 5. Dated vs. current

| Sounds like the 2000s (only for throwbacks) | Sounds like the 2020s |
|---|---|

## 6. Craft notes

- **Rhyme by vowel, in chains.** Run 4–8 lines on one ending:
  - -ío: prendío, frío, aborrecío, visto
  - -ao: pegao, sudao, amarrao, entrao
  - -á: mojá, sudá, pegá, na', ma, humedá
  - -ía: mía, prendía, todavía

  Clipped forms make rhymes easy.
- **The hook.** Make the title the hook phrase. Keep it to 4–8 lines with open vowels so the melody can lift. Repeat a 2–4 word cell. End the hook on the phrase it opened with.
- **Be specific, not poetic.** Name the time, the place, the weather, the object, the app. Avoid textbook metaphors unless they come from something in the scene.
- **One punchline per verse:** a joke, or a jab at society.
- **Callbacks.** Plant an image in verse 1 and pay it off in verse 2.
- **Ad-libs go in (parentheses):** echo the last word, or add a whispered aside.
- **Vulgarity.** One explicit image per couplet reads as sexy. Three in one line reads as comedy.
- **Premise.** Default to mutual desire between the people in the song. Cheating and third parties are worn out, and the user rejected them.

## 7. Don'ts: what makes a lyric sound fake right away

| Avoid | Why | Use instead |
|---|---|---|
| wey/güey, chido, neta, morra | Mexican | pana, brutal, veldá, gata |
| parce/parcero, mor, bacano | Colombian | pana, ma, brutal |
| klk / qué lo que, tíguere, jevi, manín, vaina | Dominican | ¿qué es la que hay?, bichote, brutal, broki |
| tío (as "dude"), mola, guay, vosotros | Spain | acho, brutal, ustedes |
| chamo/chama, burda | Venezuelan | pana, nene/nena |
| "dinero" in slang lines | sounds flat | chavos, billetes |
| textbook poetry (alma, luna llena, destino, perderse en las nubes) | sounds written, not spoken | the real object in the scene |
| five slang words in one line | sounds like a glossary | 1–2 per line |
| r → l everywhere | caricature | 1–2 times per song |
| spelling every dropped s | hard to read | only where it shapes the rhyme |

## 8. Idioms and calibration lines

### Idioms that work as lines

| Idiom | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *Te dan pon y quieres guiar* | Give them a ride and they want to drive (give an inch, they take a mile) |
| *Por encima de los gandules* | Over the top, outstanding |
| *Se fue al garete* | It went off the rails |
| *Esa es la que hay* | That's how it is |
| *Eso e' mamey* | That's easy |

### Original calibration lines (written in this voice)

- *¿Qué tú hace' despierta a la' cuatro?*
- *Se fue la luz y tú 'tás prendía*
- *Déjame en visto, pero no me pichee'*
- *Tú ere' brutal, pero ere' red flag*
- *Te di pon y ahora quiere' guiar*
- *Noventa por ciento y tú más mojá*

## 9. Scene kits

Each kit is a starting point: a real place and time, the details you can feel or hear, the words that come with it, and a few hook ideas. Pick one and make it specific. Every kit works as a story between two people with no third party.

| Scene | Details | Words | Hook ideas |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Heat wave, AC broken** (used for "Humedá") | August, 90% on the weather app, sweat, the window open and no breeze, a ceiling fan | el aire, abanico, sudá, pegá, t-shirt | humedá, el aire, sudá |
| **Apagón night** | candles, the phone at 20%, the neighbor's generator, coquíes, the barrio shouting "¡llegó la luz!" | se fue la luz, planta, a oscuras | prendía, a oscuras, que no vuelva |
| **Aguacero, hurricane season** | rain on the zinc roof, the power flickering, stuck inside, the smell of wet asphalt | aguacero, zinc, escampó, temporada | aguacero, temporada, que no escampe |
| **Beach at night: Piñones / Isla Verde** | kioskos, alcapurrias, Medalla, salt, planes flying low toward the airport, wet sand | kiosko, bacalaítos, la arena, el avión | sal, aterriza, Piñones |
| **La Placita de Santurce** | a different song from every bar, a crowd in the street, shots, perreo on the sidewalk | janguear, chinchorrear, perreo, shot | La Placita, hasta abajo, amanecer |
| **Driving the Expreso at 3 AM** | empty highway, streetlights passing, her hand on his leg, the airport exit | guiar, el guía, dar pon, la salida | pon, guía, salida |
| **Choli concert night** | after the show, voice gone, sweat, the after-party, the crowd still ringing in their ears | vendí el Choli, el corillo, la movie | el Choli, encore, sold out |
| **Bioluminescent bay** (Vieques, Fajardo, La Parguera) | water glowing blue when you move, no moon, the lancha back | la lancha, brillar, la bahía | brilla, bioluminiscente, azul |
| **Weekend in Guavate / the mountains** | lechón, music from every lechonera, fog, cold for once, the curvy road | la ruta, el monte, la neblina | neblina, la cuesta, frío |
| **Fiestas de la Calle San Sebastián** (January, Old San Juan) | cobblestones, cabezudos, plena in the street, packed crowd, sweat in January | la SanSe, plena, adoquines | la SanSe, pandero |
| **Christmas season** (the longest in the world) | parrandas at 2 AM, coquito, pasteles, the cold that isn't cold | parranda, asalto, coquito, trulla | coquito, parranda, asalto |
| **Phone life** | 3 AM FaceTime, her story, close friends, the typing bubble | story, visto, ubi, kloufrens, "escribiendo…" | ubi, escribiendo, en visto |
