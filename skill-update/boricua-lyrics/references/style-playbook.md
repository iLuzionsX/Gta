## 1. Reading a style prompt

Match the words in the prompt to writing decisions:

| If the prompt says… | Then write… |
|---|---|
| melodic, sung, falsetto, airy, high-register | Sung lines of 6–11 syllables, open vowels at the peaks, and tags like *falsete contenido* |
| rap, bars, aggressive, drill, punchy | Lines of 10–16 syllables with internal and multi-syllable rhyme, and verses of 12–16 bars |
| unhurried, spacious, minimal, ambient, reverb | A low word budget, one line per bar, a lot of repetition, and whispered intros and outros |
| party, club, perreo, dembow | Chant hooks, call-and-response, crowd ad-libs, a dance break |
| romantic, R&B, slow jam | Tender plus sensual, fewer slang words, and the same spoken word order |
| salsa, plena, bomba, jíbaro, aguinaldo | The roots forms below. These have their own structures. |
| A BPM number | **Under 80:** slow. **85–105:** the reggaetón zone. **130–150:** trap, which is usually felt half-time (65–75), so write it like a slow song unless it's rapped. |

When the prompt names a vocal (male or female, a register), put that in the delivery tags. Write the lyrics so the gender matches the singer's point of view.

## 2. Families

### Cloud trap / melodic trap / ambient

*For tone, listen to: Jhayco's Timelezz, Mora's Paraíso, Rauw Alejandro's Saturno*

- **Structure:**
  1. `[Intro – susurrado]`: 2 lines
  2. `[Verso 1]`: 6 lines
  3. `[Pre-Coro]`: 2 lines
  4. `[Coro]`: 8 lines
  5. `[Verso 2]`: 6 lines
  6. `[Coro]`: 8 lines
  7. `[Outro]`: 2–3 lines
- **Lines:** 6–11 syllables, about one line per bar.
- **Hook:** a hypnotic loop with falsetto-friendly vowels, ending where it started.
- **Fits:** night, heat, haze, intimacy, being high, flex vs. feeling.
- **Budget:** 60–75 words per minute. The approved example, "Humedá", is in this family.
- **Alternate layouts:** "Humedá" and "Brilla" both used the default layout above. When the song log shows it's been used, switch to one of these:
  - **Hook first:** `[Coro]` → `[Verso 1]` → `[Coro]` → `[Puente]` (4 lines, half-time or spoken) → `[Coro]` → `[Outro]`
  - **Post-coro tag:** `[Intro]` → `[Verso 1]` → `[Coro]` → `[Post-Coro]` (a 2-line chant after the hook) → `[Verso 2]` → `[Coro]` → `[Post-Coro]`, then end cold, with no outro
  - **One long verse:** `[Intro]` → `[Coro]` → `[Verso]` (10–12 lines that build) → `[Coro]` → `[Outro]` (the hook slowed down and cut short)
  - **Two voices:** `[Verso 1 – él]` → `[Coro]` → `[Verso 2 – ella, susurrado]` → `[Coro – los dos]`, where her lines come in as (parenthetical) backing vocals
- **Alternate hook shapes:** instead of one word echoed in (parentheses), use a 2–4 word phrase (*No me suelte' el guía*), a PR question (*¿Qué tú vas a hacer?*), a line of dialogue the song keeps quoting, or an English tag set against a Spanish answer.
- **Alternate intros:** a sound cue in the tag (`[Intro – nota de voz, ruido de carro]`), a spoken line in the middle of a conversation, or no intro at all, with the first line on the beat.

### Reggaetón / perreo

- **Structure:**
  1. `[Intro – ad-libs]`
  2. `[Coro]`
  3. `[Verso 1]`: 8–12 lines
  4. `[Pre-Coro]`
  5. `[Coro]`
  6. `[Verso 2]`: 8–12 lines
  7. `[Puente – break]`: a chant
  8. `[Coro]`
  9. `[Outro]`
- **Lines:** 8–12 syllables, riding the dembow (*boom-ch-boom-chick*).
- **Hook:** chantable, in 2–4 word cells. Use call-and-response: the lead sings a line and the answer goes in (parentheses).
- **Words:** perreo, hasta abajo, bellaca, pegao, janguear, shot, la disco.
- **Budget:** 80–100 words per minute.
- **Note:** "dembow" in PR talk means the reggaetón beat. Dominican dembow is its own genre. Keep PR slang unless the user asks for a Dominican voice.

### Hard trap / drill / rap

- **Structure:**
  1. `[Coro]`: 4–8 lines
  2. `[Verso 1]`: 16 bars
  3. `[Coro]`
  4. `[Verso 2]`: 16 bars
  5. `[Coro]`
- **Lines:** 10–16 syllables with multi-syllable and internal rhyme. A punchline every 2–4 bars, and ad-libs (brr, prr, ey).
- **Words:** bichote, tiraera, chavos, prendas, guille, corillo, meterle, romper.
- **Budget:** 120–160 words per minute.

### R&B / romántico / slow jam

- **Structure:**
  1. `[Verso 1]`
  2. `[Pre-Coro]`
  3. `[Coro]`
  4. `[Verso 2]`
  5. `[Pre-Coro]`
  6. `[Coro]`
  7. `[Puente]`
  8. `[Coro]`
- **Lines:** sung, with long vowels and room for runs.
- **Tone:** tender and sensual. Use fewer slang words but keep the spoken word order (*¿qué tú quieres?*).
- **Budget:** 60–80 words per minute.

### Pop urbano / ballad / bolero

- **Structure:** verse, pre-chorus, chorus, verse, chorus, bridge, chorus.
- **Language:** clean by default.
- **PR flavor:** comes from places, word order and a few words (acho, pana, ma) more than from slang.
- **Bolero:** allows a little more classic romance, but keep it anchored to a real object or place.

### Salsa

- **Structure:**
  1. The **cuerpo**, verses that tell the story.
  2. The **montuno**, where a short `[Coro]` repeats and `[Pregón]` lines (*soneos*) answer it.
  3. A `[Mambo]` horn break.
  4. More coro and pregón.
  5. The ending.
- **Pregones:** should sound improvised, witty and local, with shout-outs to barrios.
- **Budget:** 80–110 words per minute. The coro repeats many times, which is what fills the time.

### Plena

- **What it is:** "el periódico cantado", the sung newspaper. It tells the news of the barrio: a current event, a scandal, a joke.
- **Structure:** short solo verses (4 lines) alternating with a short coro (2 lines). Driven by panderos and güiro.
- **Fits:** humor and social commentary, such as LUMA, the blackouts, gentrification or the tapón (traffic).

### Bomba

- **Structure:** call-and-response. The solista sings a line and the coro answers with a short repeated refrain.
- **Context:** a dialogue between the dancer and the lead drum (the primo or subidor).
- **Lyrics:** few words, rhythmic and percussive, with a lot of repetition.

### Música jíbara (seis, aguinaldo, controversia)

- **Form:** décima espinela, 10 lines of 8 syllables that rhyme ABBAACCDDC.
- **Imagery:** rural (cafetal, monte, flamboyán, the cuatro).
- **Aguinaldo:** the Christmas form, about parrandas, coquito and pasteles.
- **Controversia:** two trovadores dueling in improvised décimas, the jíbaro ancestor of a tiraera.

### Afro-reggaetón, dance, EDM and anything else

- Keep the PR voice and adapt the density:
  - **Afro-leaning:** bouncy, short lines.
  - **House or EDM:** mostly hook, often 60–100 words in total.
- For any style not listed, map it to the nearest family by tempo and by how much is sung vs. rapped, then adjust.

## 3. Suno / Udio formatting

- **Where things go:** paste the style text into **Style** and the lyrics into **Lyrics**.
- **Section tags:** use square brackets, with a delivery note after an en dash: `[Coro – falsete contenido]`, `[Intro – susurrado]`. The approved song used Spanish tags. If the model ignores a section, switch to English tags (`[Verse]`, `[Chorus]`, `[Bridge]`).
- **Parentheses** come out as backing vocals, echoes or ad-libs.
- **Repeats:** write every chorus out in full instead of "repeat chorus".
- **Stage directions** belong in the tags, not in the sung lines.
- **Fixes after a render:**
  - Falsetto too strong: add "breathy, soft falsetto" to the tag.
  - Words rushed: cut words; don't change the tempo.
  - A word mispronounced: respell it the way it's said (*humedá*, *pol*).
